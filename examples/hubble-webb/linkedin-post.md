# Hubble’s colors are a translation, not a snapshot

Hubble images turn invisible light into something we can interpret.

Hubble records light in shades of gray through filters that select different wavelengths. Image processors assign colors to those filtered images: the longest wavelength becomes red, the middle one green, and the shortest blue.

For example, narrowband filters can isolate light from hydrogen, oxygen, and sulfur. Coloring those observations helps show where these elements appear, even though the colors aren’t necessarily what our eyes would see.

A practical lesson for designers and content creators: when you use color to explain data, make clear what the colors represent.
