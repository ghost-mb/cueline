# Chat answer

**Question:** Are the colors in Hubble and Webb images real? Explain how they are chosen.

**Not always.** Hubble records images in grayscale through filters, and processors assign colors to the filtered images to make features easier to interpret. For broadband filters that capture visible wavelengths, the colors can be a direct translation of what human eyes might see; for narrowband filters or light outside the visible spectrum, the colors are representative rather than literal. [[S1 0:52]](https://youtu.be/QGf0yzdM5OA?t=52) [[S1 1:23]](https://youtu.be/QGf0yzdM5OA?t=83) [[S1 2:07]](https://youtu.be/QGf0yzdM5OA?t=127)

For Hubble, processors typically map the longest-wavelength filtered image to red, the middle wavelength to green, and the shortest to blue, then combine them. Narrowband images can highlight elements such as hydrogen, oxygen, and sulfur; infrared and ultraviolet views may also be colorized to show information our eyes cannot see.
