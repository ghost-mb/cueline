# Make one useful piece of content easier to consume

**Subject line options**
1. One idea, more than one format
2. Let customers choose how they consume your content
3. You don’t have to start with every channel

**Preview text:** A practical way to extend one piece of content without trying to be everywhere.

Daniel Rosehill’s point is simple: people may care more about useful content from a business they trust than whether it comes from a blog, podcast, or YouTube channel. For a small business, that doesn’t mean you need to publish everywhere. It means you can make one good idea available in more than one format.

## **Start with the content, then choose where it can go.**

Many businesses begin by asking which channel to use. Rosehill suggests thinking about how the same content could work across formats instead. A blog post might also be useful as audio or video. A video could have a written version.

You still need to choose where to distribute it. Rosehill notes that businesses can’t be everywhere and do every channel well. The opportunity is to widen distribution by adapting content you already have, rather than treating each format as a separate project.

## **Make it easier for people to find their preferred format.**

The goal is to give people options for consuming your content. Rosehill describes linking those formats together: embed audio or video in a blog post, or link from a video to its text or podcast version.

That way, someone who finds your content in one place can also find another way to use it. You’re not starting from scratch each time; you’re connecting the versions of the same idea.

## **Try this week: repurpose one recent piece.**

Choose a blog post, video, or other piece of content you’ve already made. Pick one additional format that feels manageable. For example, turn a blog post into a short audio recording, or write a text version of a video.

Then add a link between the two versions so readers or viewers can find the format that suits them. Keep the experiment small: one piece, one additional format, one clear connection.

Rosehill argues this approach can help small businesses widen distribution without large marketing resources. The key is to explore what works for your business, not to take on every channel at once.
